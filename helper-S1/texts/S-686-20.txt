The intradermal local analgesic action of prilocaine
      A controlled double-blind comparison with lignocaine and procaine

                A D R I A N P A D F I E L D , MB, BS, DA*
                 Aylwen Bursar, Department of Pharmacology
              St Bartholomew’s Hospital Medical College, London


In 1959 Eriksson and Gordh 1 reported the local analgesic activity of
L67 - prilocaine (Citanest) and claimed that intradermally it had a
longer duration of action than lignocaine. This view was based on
findings which did not reach a level of statistical significance. A fur-
ther paper by Eriksson2 in 1961 produced the same figures and also
stated that prilocaine had a longer duration of action than lignocaine
when employed for finger, caudal or spinal blocks.
   Berling and Bjorn3 compared prilocaine and lignocaine with and
without adrenaline in dental local analgesia and found that prilocaine
had a shorter duration of action, especially in soft tissues, which is
generally regarded as an advantage. Similar findings were made by
Cowan4 and by Goldman and Gray 5.
   Further trials6 using peripheral nerve blocks, spinal and epidural
blockade showed prilocaine to have a longer duration of local
analgesic action than lignocaine but both had similar duration of
action when each was tested with added adrenaline (1 :250,000).
Bromage7 observed that, for epidural anzsthesia, lignocaine had a
shorter duration of action than prilocaine or mepivacaine but the
addition of adrenaline (1 :200,000) to all three agents prolonged their
effects so that all acted for similar durations.
   Two recent papers819 comparing the actions of prilocaine and
lignocaine topically reported opposing results, the former suggesting
that prilocaine lasted longer while the latter indicated that it had an
action of duration similar to that of lignocaine.
   Studies on systemic effects and on toxicities showed that prilocaine
was half as toxic as lignocaine in man 10 while in animals 1 1 it was half
as active in lowering blood pressure and stimulating respiration and
had less vasodilating action.
   In view of the divergent reports of the duration of action of prilo-
caine compared with the more familiar agents procaine and ligno-

caine, further work is necessary. As part of their practical work in
pharmacology at this Medical College, medical and dental students
receive intradermal injections of local analgesic drugs into the skin
of their forearms and assess the intensity and duration of actions of
each and compared with an injection of 0.9% sodium chloride
solution. A group of 40 dental students participated in a double blind
controlled trial as indicated below with a view to assessing the activity
and duration of action of procaine, lignocaine and prilocaine.

                               METHOD


The double blind, controlled trial was carried out during a series of
practical classes by twenty pairs of clinical dental students. It was
based upon a class experiment design used for many years in the
Department 12 which was adapted from that of Mongar13.
   The drugs used, prilocaine (2 %), lignocaine (2 %), procaine (2 %)
(all without an added vasoconstrictor agent) and 0.9% sodium
chloride solution were kindly supplied by the St Bartholomew’s
Hospital Dispensary in identical numbered bottles. The numbering
code was not revealed to the author, who gave the injections, or to
the students until the experiment had been completed.
   The author injected 0.2ml of each solution intradermally into the
forearm skin of each subject after sterilization by washing in a stream
of 70% ethanol. The injection sites were numbered to identify the
blebs which had been raised on the palmar aspect of each left forearm.
The sites of injection among the students followed a random alloca-
tion following a Latin Square design.
   As soon as possible after the injections at 0 min. the partner of each
subject tested the blebs for pain in response to a prick from a sharp
sterile needle which aroused a distinct painful sensation in normal
skin. With the subject’s eyes averted, each bleb was tested six times
and the number of non-painful stimuli out of six were recorded and
indicated the degree of analgesia. This assessment was repeated at 5,
10, 15,30 and 45 minutes.

   I am most grateful to Miss Catherine Duncan who advised on the
statistical design of the experiment and carried out the following
 statistical analysis.
   An analysis of variance was carried out to test subject differences.
The latter was divided into two parts, one to test the difference
between the saline solution and the analgesic agents and the other to
determine whether there were any differential effects among the three
analgesic drugs. Differences between subjects were expected and were
significant (p -=0.01) but no effect was found due to varying the site of
application. Saline showed significantly less activity than the com-
bined analgesic agents (p < 0.001) and the differences between the
three analgesic drugs were highly significant (p < 0.001).
   To find out where these differences occurred, further analysis was
carried out on the means for the three analgesic drugs using Hart-
ley’s14 method for multiple comparisons among means. This is
equivalent to Student’st-test but is applicableto three or more means.
Hartley’s test takes into account the ranking of the two means in any
comparison. (Student’s t-test would be incorrect in such a case tend-
ing to over estimate the significance).
   With Hartley’s test on the Total Activity scores are as follows:
      Prilocaine 2 % was more active than lignocaine 2 % (p < 0.05)
      Prilocaine 2 % was more active than procaine 2 % (p < 0.001)
      Lignocaine 2 % was more active than procaine 2 % (p < 0.05)
   It was thought worthwhile to look at the individual times, es-
pecially the later stages of the experiment, to see whether there was
any evidence of differential effects among the drugs. The analyses of
variance given in table 2 should be assessed with some reservation
because of heterogeneity of variance. (Heterogeneity arises when the
mean value approaches either the maximum possible or minimum
possible scores.)

   Vascular efects
Apart from testing for analgesia, subjects and partners were asked to
comment on any vascular phenomena associated with the blebs, suchas
blanching or flushing. Of the twenty pairs, eighteen noticed vaso-
constriction occurring with priIocaine relative to the saline control
and vasodilatation was noted by twelve with procaine.
  The blanching produced by prilocaine alone was less marked than
that caused by adrenaline 1 :200,000 (personal observation).

                                   SUMMARY
A controlled double blind trial has shown that, intradermally, prilo-
caine 2% was a significantly more active local analgesic agent than
lignocaine 2 % and procaine 2 %when total activity is measured over
a 45 minute period as well as 30 and 45 minutes after its adminis-
tration.
   Vasoconstriction was produced by prilocaine alone which is less
marked than that following adrenaline 1:200,000.
