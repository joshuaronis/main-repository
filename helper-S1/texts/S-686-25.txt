   INTRADERMAL STUDY OF A NEW LOCAL ANAESTHETIC AGENT:
                        APTOCAINE
                           F. REYNOLDS, T. H. L. BRYSON AND A. D. G. NICHOLAS

                                                         SUMMARY

           In a double-blind trial in 28 human volunteers, a new local anaesthetic agent, aptocaine, was com-
           pared intradermally at 1, 2 and 3% concentrations with lignocaine 2% and bupivacaine 0.5%. In a
           second trial in 27 subjects, 1% aptocaine was compared with mepivacaine and prilocaine, both 1, 2
           and 3 %. In terms of activity as determined by area of anaesthesia, and of duration of action, aptocaine
           was similar to mepivacaine and more active and long-lasting than lignocaine and prilocaine. By this

           route aptocaine also appeared longer-lasting than bupivacaine. Duration of action was unaffected by
           concentration. Aptocaine had marked vasoconstrictor activity, which was maximal at 1%. These
           local anaesthetic properties suggest that aptocaine merits clinical trials, especially in dentistry.
