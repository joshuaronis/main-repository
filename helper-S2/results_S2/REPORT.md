# S2 results: 15 papers on the safety side of injected and topical anaesthetics

All 15 papers in the S2 packet are the papers requested, and each was read in full. Eight claims change verdict on this evidence. Six move from narrowed to refuted: C090, D013, C050, C057, C063 and D067, and D067's refutation holds only if complex regional pain syndrome type I counts as neuropathic pain. A054 moves from confirmed to refuted, and D072 from confirmed to narrowed. The other 36 claims re-judged keep their verdicts, most of them with proposed wording that stays true on the evidence. The papers, together with 68 further studies they led to, also produce 103 findings on 33 Notion pages, 13 of them high importance. Three things are not done. The epinephrine amount Wang 2011 injected is not recorded, because the write carrying it was stopped by the content filter and, under the hand-over's rule, was not retried. Judgements that need a full text no free copy provided stay open, chiefly D033, which turns on Vlassakov 2012. Claims whose subject is a dose ceiling, a toxicity threshold or a preparation step were counted, not judged (listed under Problems).

## What each paper settled

- **S-686-37 Wang 2011.** The epinephrine was one intraplantar injection into the mouse hind paw, not Khasar's intradermal injection into the rat paw dorsum. Hyperalgesia lasted 3 to 4 days in wild-type mice and about 21 days with low sensory-neuron GRK2, and a lower epinephrine amount in the same experiment gave a shorter window. So the window's length has been measured since 1999, at two amounts, and C090 is refuted.
- **S-686-36 Wang 2008.** No skin nerve-fibre result exists for lidocaine with epinephrine alone. PGP9.5-stained fibres were counted, with no loss against saline about a day after a single subcutaneous injection in rats, only in skin given lidocaine with epinephrine plus the experimental adjuvant; haematoxylin-and-eosin sections at about six days showed intact skin. D013 is refuted, C046 stays narrowed, and the "nobody has measured damage in skin" statement is wrong in five places on four pages.
- **S-686-42 Alam 2010.** A prospective cohort with 19 analysed: repeated infiltration of one Mohs site within one session, with six serum draws over up to about eight hours. It is not a session of many small skin deposits, so D069 stays confirmed. It is, beside Sato 2022, a second study of serum levels after repeated infiltration within one session, which D072's reason does not list. Its DOI is 10.1016/j.jaad.2009.08.046.
- **S-686-47 Troullos 1987.** Fifteen young healthy oral-surgery patients in open parallel groups (10 given lidocaine with epinephrine, 5 plain mepivacaine), with no cardiac patients; the epinephrine given was above the conventional cardiac-patient limit. D074 stays narrowed, and a search it prompted adds two studies in cardiovascular or hypertensive patients (Davenport 1990, Meechan 1997).
- **S-686-44 Klingenström 1964.** Nine healthy human subjects, subcutaneous forearm infiltration, with tissue oxygen tension read at 1.5, 2.5 and 3.5 hours and lower at the adrenaline and noradrenaline sites than at the felypressin site. That is a human measurement over hours twenty years before Miller 1984, but not at a wheal, so C084 stays narrowed.
- **S-686-38 Wiesmann 2018.** Pigs, with each solution applied onto the exposed median nerve; epinephrine was given only with bupivacaine and at a higher concentration than the one C074 names, so C074 stays confirmed. Nerve oxygen saturation with epinephrine fell over the first half hour and recovered by one hour, which corrects two high-importance sentences on the Lidocaine With Epinephrine Wheals page. Studies it led to show that dexamethasone is not vessel-free: it blanches human skin and lowered rat nerve blood flow.
- **S-686-27 Schmittner 2010.** It cites Guay five times and adopts the general limit Guay's own summary concludes with (Guay 2009), so it does not contradict Guay; C071 stays narrowed with its uncertainty cleared. It is a cytokine study of subcutaneous tumescent prilocaine in 30 patients, not an intradermal or methaemoglobin-outcome study.
- **S-686-23 Rames 2026.** Liposomal bupivacaine was given by local infiltration of the surgical site; the only tissue plane named is subcutaneous, and "intradermal" never appears. D048 stays confirmed with its uncertainty cleared. The page's statement that no other product in the sustained-release class is marketed is outdated: two other extended-release bupivacaine products are approved (Blair 2021; Scott 2024).
- **S-686-07 Effendy 2015.** Plasma lidocaine and prilocaine did not accumulate over ten days of daily topical application, and methaemoglobin was never measured, so C070's wording credits the study with a result it does not hold. No patient is described as neuropathic, so the study does not bear on D066, whose refutation rests on Moghtaderi 2009.
- **S-686-48 Varghese 2024.** Ten studies (eight called randomised), reported as 892 children. By the paper's own pooling, amethocaine was favoured on child-reported absence of pain (risk ratio 0.58, 95% CI 0.41 to 0.82) and first-attempt cannulation (risk ratio 0.98, 0.97 to 0.99), with no difference on four other outcomes. D068 stays refuted, and the Reference and Product Guide wording on what replaced the withdrawn Cochrane review needs updating.
- **S-686-43 Flondell 2017.** Five of the eleven items in the symptom severity scale concern pain, but the paper reports only the total, with no separate pain outcome; the cream went on the forearm to guide sensory relearning and did not differ from placebo. D067 stays narrowed on this paper, and a search it prompted found Strauss 2015, which refutes D067 under the two conditions given under Verdict changes.
- **S-686-05 Cui 2017.** The steroid was methylprednisolone, given with ropivacaine in a short course of closely spaced sessions, so C028 and D071 stay narrowed with their uncertainty cleared. Its randomised intracutaneous ropivacaine–steroid injections with pain outcomes make "no wheal study of ropivacaine with a pain outcome exists" wrong on five product rows. Searches it prompted refute A054 (Xu 2013) and narrow D072 (Nguyen 2021).
- **S-686-18 Mülkoğlu 2020.** No skin anaesthesia is documented: pain and itch scores only, in an uncontrolled series with 20 analysed. D033 stays narrowed and uncertain, because it turns on whether the eight papers of Vlassakov's 2012 analysis include Attal 1999. Several Reference and product-page statements about "the one wheal study" need correcting.
- **S-686-01 Baroni 2013.** It cites Hillerup 2011 (reference 28) as support for the neurotoxicity hypothesis and concludes that articaine is not toxic to nerve structure, so "none of them contradicts it" fails and C050 is refuted. Its injection went into the mucosa beside the mental nerve, which the articaine page describes as injection into the nerve.
- **S-686-12 Hopman 2017.** It does not cite the rat sciatic-nerve study: its only Hillerup reference is the 2011 Danish registry paper. As a two-database systematic review of articaine neurotoxicity, it refutes "the one systematic attempt" (C057), and the cell-culture studies it reviews refute C063.

## Verdict changes

- **C090** narrowed → refuted. Source: Wang 2011 (S-686-37, PMID 21514055). The Khasar 1999 extra-study entry (PMID 10085337) narrows it on its own: Khasar reported a dose-dependent hyperalgesia, not a single amount.
- **D013** narrowed → refuted. Source: Wang 2008 (S-686-36, PMID 18806059). The measurement is in rats, after a single injection containing an experimental adjuvant; none of those limits is an element D013 states.
- **C050** narrowed → refuted. Source: Baroni 2013 (S-686-01, PMID 22458536). Baroni contradicts the neurotoxicity conclusion with a different design and does not test Hillerup's intraneural measurement; the Hopman 2017 entry stays narrowed because Hopman does not cite the study. The count of twenty-nine citing papers was not checked.
- **C057** narrowed → refuted. Source: Hopman 2017 (S-686-12, PMID 28972589).
- **C063** narrowed → refuted. Sources: Hopman 2017 (S-686-12) and Werdehausen 2009 (PMID 19700777): articaine killed neuronal cells in culture, outside any nerve sheath.
- **A054** confirmed → refuted. Source: Xu 2013 (PMID 23566267), reached from Cui 2017: oral methylcobalamin and repeated subcutaneous lidocaine were randomised arms of one trial in herpetic neuralgia.
- **D067** narrowed → refuted, uncertain. Source: Strauss 2015 (PMID 26270587), reached from Flondell 2017: a double-blind placebo-cream crossover in 12 people with CRPS type I, with a pain outcome. It holds only if CRPS type I counts as neuropathic pain (the D067 searches counted it; the IASP definition does not) and if a crossover whose session order the authors call pseudorandomised counts as randomised. The Flondell 2017 and Flondell 2024 entries stay narrowed.
- **D072** confirmed → narrowed. Sources: Cui 2017 (S-686-05) and Nguyen 2021 (PMID 33855194). No study measures clearance between sessions, so the absence holds; the page's description of how far apart published sessions are spaced does not. The Alam 2010 and Effendy 2015 entries stay confirmed.

## High-importance Notion findings

- **S2-F001** 💉 Lidocaine With Epinephrine Wheals: "has not been measured since" — the hyperalgesia window has been measured again, in mice, at two amounts (Wang 2011).
- **S2-F003** same page: "Beta-2 sensitization is a two-hour effect" — in mice the same beta-2-mediated hyperalgesia lasted days (Wang 2011), and the page's concealment argument rests on the two-hour figure.
- **S2-F011** same page: "the one study that measured skin oxygen for hours" — a human forearm study measured it over 3.5 hours, with a smaller fall (Klingenström 1964).
- **S2-F015** same page: "oxygenation with epinephrine tracked saline" — true at one hour only; nerve oxygen saturation fell over the first half hour, severely in two of ten nerves (Wiesmann 2018).
- **S2-F016** same page: "oxygen held up where it was measured at a nerve with flow halved" — the same early fall (Wiesmann 2018).
- **S2-F022** adjuvants-without-a-vasoconstrictor, recommendation: "Dexamethasone and magnesium are the only two agents here with no action on a blood vessel at all" — dexamethasone blanches human skin (Andersen 1993) and lowered rat nerve blood flow (Shishido 2002), and magnesium injected into skin caused a flare (Ushida 2009).
- **S2-F039** Product Guide, rank 19 (EMLA): the evidence inventory omits a randomised four-week daily-use trial in carpal tunnel syndrome (Moghtaderi 2009).
- **S2-F057** Ropiconest, lower strength, Farmacia La Paz: "no wheal study of ropivacaine with a pain outcome exists" — randomised trials of intracutaneous ropivacaine with methylprednisolone and pain outcomes exist in acute zoster (Cui 2017, Cui 2018); the row's verdict and its "what would change this" line rest on the sentence.
- **S2-F058** Ropiconest, higher strength, Farmacia La Paz: the same sentence.
- **S2-F059** Ropiconest, higher strength, Curitek: the same sentence.
- **S2-F060** Naropin, Vitau: the same sentence.
- **S2-F061** Ropiconest, higher strength, Vitau: the same sentence.
- **S2-F067** Product Guide, Injectables: "One finding governs the whole section. No randomised trial of cutaneous or subcutaneous local anaesthetic infiltration for established chronic peripheral neuropathic pain was located" — randomised trials with locally infiltrated lidocaine arms in postherpetic neuralgia exist (Xiao 2010, Amjad 2005).

## Files and conventions

- `results_S2/` holds the merged results in the hand-over's formats:
  - `claim_updates.json`, `extra_studies.json`, `notion_findings.json` and `papers_status.csv`.
  - `paper_cards/<paper_id>.json`, one card per paper. Each card's `studies_it_cites_that_matter` lists every cited study that bears on a topic in the pages, with PMIDs, for checking against S1's W- claims.
- **Page numbers** in every `page` and `page_in_paper` field are PDF page numbers, as marked in `texts/<paper_id>.txt`; `0` means a PubMed abstract. Each text file gives every page twice: in reading order, where quotes come from, and as a `pdftotext -layout` view for tables. Ligatures are written out as plain letters. Flondell 2017's PDF opens with a publisher cover page, so its article starts on PDF page 2. Klingenström 1964 and Troullos 1987 are scans with an OCR text layer, checked against the page images where numbers mattered.
- **Quotes from an extra study** have `paper_id` `""` and the study's `pmid`, and are checked against the cached abstract. A Notion finding that rests on an extra study has the same pair and a `pmid` key, which the hand-over's finding schema does not name.
- **Finding ids** run S2-F001 to S2-F103 in the hand-over's paper order. Three Notion sentences were each flagged from two papers, and each such finding's `notes` names its pair: S2-F063 and S2-F077, S2-F069 and S2-F083, S2-F091 and S2-F100.
- **Verdict per entry.** An extra-study entry never sits below the claim's standing verdict; where a source on its own would only narrow a claim already refuted, the entry keeps the standing verdict and its `reason` says what the source corrects. Entries whose verdict or wording was set on review end their `reason` or `notes` with a "Review note (orchestrating session)".
- `pm_work/parts/<paper_id>/` holds each paper's working files: its parts before merging, its `status.json` and a `notes.md`. The notes record the claim screen, every PubMed and Notion search, every page fetched with its `as of` version, and the verbatim sentences relied on. `pm_work/parts/finding_id_map.json` maps each provisional finding id to its final one.
- `pm_work/tools/` holds the scripts used: `pmcache.py`, `s2check.py` (schema, quote and dose-figure checks), `claims_view.py` (claims with dose figures masked), `merge_parts.py` and `report_counts.py`.
