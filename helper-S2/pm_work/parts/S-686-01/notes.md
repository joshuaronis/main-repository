## 2. What the paper is, and the own question

- Design: controlled animal experiment (split-mouth: treated right side vs saline left side), 24 male Wistar rats,
  3 groups of 8: articaine with epinephrine (G1), lidocaine with epinephrine (G2), epinephrine alone (G3); the
  formulations are the standard dental ones (figures in the paper, PDF p3; not copied here). Single injection into the
  alveolar mucosa beside the lower right first molar, near the mental nerve (PDF p3, Figure 1) — NOT into the nerve;
  saline into the contralateral mucosa. Killed at 24 h; mandibles decalcified, serial H&E sections where the mental
  nerve crosses the cortical bone; light microscopy; qualitative 1–5 score of leucocyte infiltration "and/or any
  possible necrosis area"; Kruskal-Wallis and Dunn tests (PDF p3). Funding CNPq; no conflicts declared.
- Results (PDF p5): "The mental nerve was not histologically affected by the treatments." Epinephrine alone gave higher
  connective-tissue inflammation scores (p < 0.05) than either anaesthetic. Table I (PDF p3) medians: articaine treated
  3 vs control 2; lidocaine 2 vs 2; epinephrine 4 vs 2 (score 3 = "light", 4 = "moderate"; the abstract and Fig. 3
  legend call the articaine infiltrate "moderate" — an internal inconsistency). Discussion: "no significant increase
  in score inflammation was observed 24 h after articaine injection" (p5).
- Conclusion (abstract p1 and p5): "The results suggest that articaine is not toxic to the nervous structure ...".
- No stated limitations section; the conclusion itself says "further studies are necessary".

### Own question: does Baroni cite Hillerup 2011, and how does it present its result?
- Hillerup 2011 = ref [28] (PDF p6): "Hillerup S, Bakke M, Larsen JO, Thomsen CE, Gerds TA. Concentration-dependent
  neurotoxicity of articaine: an electrophysiological and stereological study of the rat sciatic nerve. Anesth Analg
  2011;112:1330–8." PubMed confirms PMID 21467556 (Anesth Analg 2011;112(6):1330-8, DOI 10.1213/ANE.0b013e3182172a2e;
  search 'Hillerup S[Author] AND articaine AND sciatic nerve' = 1 hit; lookup_article_by_citation also returns
  21467556). Publication types in the record: Journal Article; Research Support, Non-U.S. Gov't (no Retracted
  Publication type).
- Only one in-text citation of [28] (all bracketed citations listed and checked): PDF p5, Discussion:
  "So, it was suggested that an important cause of paresthesia could be related to the local anesthetic dosedependent
  neurotoxicity. Some in vitro studies support this hypothesis [27,28]."  -> Baroni groups Hillerup (an in vivo rat
  intraneural study) with Werdehausen 2009 [27] (neuroblastoma cells) as "in vitro studies" — a mis-description.
  Hillerup's methods, results, injection site, nerve or concentrations are never described.
- Then (p5): "Unlike studies that support the hypothesis that articaine has neurotoxic potential, Leuschner and Leblanc
  [23] documented no pathomorphological systemic changes ..." and "Similarly, in the present study no significant
  increase in score inflammation was observed 24 h after articaine injection. Microscopic analysis of nervous structure
  showed no difference between the treated and the control sides."
- And (p5): "Although the higher incidence of paresthesia associated to the use of [dose figure omitted] articaine
  formulation in comparison to other local anesthetics [10–13], the hypothesis that this local anesthetic may be
  neurotoxic was not confirmed in the present study."
- Answer: Baroni cites Hillerup 2011 once, only as support for the dose-dependent-neurotoxicity hypothesis. It presents
  its own result as not confirming that hypothesis and lines itself up with Leuschner "unlike studies that support" it,
  so it sets its conclusion against the hypothesis Hillerup is cited for — but it never names Hillerup when stating its
  result, never says it contradicts or fails to replicate Hillerup, and does not discuss the design differences:
  injection site (mucosa beside the nerve vs intraneural), nerve (mental vs sciatic), concentration (one marketed
  formulation vs a concentration series), outcome and time (qualitative H&E inflammation score at 24 h vs evoked
  potentials and stereology of fibre area and myelin thickness at 3 weeks). Its finding (no nerve injury after an
  extraneural injection) is compatible with Hillerup's; its stated conclusion ("articaine is not toxic to the nervous
  structure") is the opposite of Hillerup's conclusion.
- Hillerup & Jensen 2006 = ref [13] (PMID 16343853, Danish case series of 52 patients with injection nerve injuries
  after mandibular block). Cited four times: p2 "[10–13]" (increase in paresthesia reports with articaine and
  prilocaine), p2 "[13]" ("the nerve damage could be permanent"), p5 "Several authors [10–13]", p5 "[10–13]" in the
  sentence above. Always as epidemiological background for the paresthesia signal, never contradicted.
- Baroni does not cite the other 2011 Hillerup paper (Hillerup, Jensen & Ersbøll, JADA 2011, PMID 21531935, Danish
  registry "needle lesion or neurotoxicity?").
- Other citation problems inside Baroni (card notes): ref [3] and ref [12] are the same paper (Garisto 2010); ref [31]
  (Radwan 2002, PMID 11812691) is cited for "prilocaine and lidocaine at the same concentration presented equivalent
  neurotoxicity in rats", but Radwan studied lidocaine, bupivacaine, mepivacaine and ropivacaine on chick-embryo DRG
  neurons in vitro (no prilocaine, no rats); ref [35] (Ribeiro 2003, PMID 14959905) is described as "subcutaneous
  articaine injection in rats", but Ribeiro implanted solution-soaked paper cones in polyethylene tubes subcutaneously.
