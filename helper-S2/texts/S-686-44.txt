The use of adrenaline and noradrenaline as vasoconstrictors in local anaesthesia may produce untoward effects. Other agents have therefore been tested,
for example, phenylalanine,-Iysine8-vasopressin,which is known as Octapressin@,Sandoz. We have recently compared changes in the local colour
of the skin following the administration of adrenaline and Octapressin when
and L. WESTERMARK
used as vasoconstrictors in lidocaine (P. KLINCENSTROM
(1963p). After the initial pallor, local cyanosis was evident centrally in the
pale zone following adrenaline, but not after Octapressin. This cyanosis is a
sign of local hypoxia, possibly leading to increased tissue acidity, and thus
to a dilatation of capillaries and venules, in which blood with deoxygenated
haemoglobin is pooled. I n our opinion, the local hypoxia is caused by high
oxygen consumption induced by the locally injected adrenaline. If this be so,
the local tissue-oxygen tension should be lower after adrenaline than after
Octapressin. By measuring local tissue acidity or local tissue-oxygen tension
it would be possible to verify the above-mentioned hypothesis. The electrodes
for pH measurement, however, traumatize the tissue much more than do
the PO, electrodes. Therefore, we chose the method of PO, measurements.
The aim of our investigationwas to ascertain, by a direct measuring method,
the difference in the tissue-oxygen tension in zones in which the local anaesthetic containing either of these vasoconstrictors was injected. We also tested
zones with noradrenaline as vasoconstrictor.

M A T E R I A L AND M E T H O D S
The investigation was performed on the forearms of nine healthy human
subjects. On the volar side of each forearm 0.5 ml of anaesthetic solution was
injected immediately subcutaneously in three different places : distally 0.5
per cent. lidocaine with noradrenaline 1 :200,000, proximally 0.5 per cent.

RESULTS
Colour changes.-A pale area, 3-4 cm in diameter, was seen within some
minutes at, and around, the site of each injection; this occurred somewhat
sooner after adrenaline and noradrenaline. The pallor was approximately of
the same intensity after the application of all three agents, but slightly more
widespread after Octapressin. During the first hour after the injections local
cyanosis developed centrally in the adrenaline and the noradrenaline zones,
but a narrow halo of 3 4 mm at the periphery of these zones remained pale.
Following the administration of Octapressin, the pallor persisted unchanged
for 3.5 hours or longer, and no cyanosis appeared.
Oxygen-tensiondiference.-With very few exceptions, the tissue-oxygen tension
at the injection sites was lower after adrenaline and noradrenaline, and varied
between 50 and 90 per cent. of that after Octapressin. The results are given
in table 1. The 0, tension outside the infiltrated zones was measured in three
cases, and was found to be the same as in the Octapressin zones.
Statistics.-Cases 1-6 in table 1 were subjected to statistical analysis, since
they were tested on both forearms 1.5, 2.5 and 3.5 hours after injection. The
experimental error was estimated by variance analysis. All the values in the
adrenaline and the noradrenaline zones of these subjects were changed in the
same direction ; consequently, it proved unnecessary to apply a significance
test. The tendencies of the values in cases 7, 8 and 9 were the same as those
in the other cases. The standard deviation for the adrenaline and the noradrenaline values was 9.3 and 8.0 per cent., respectively. Thus, the standard

S U M MARY
The local tissue-oxygen tension in the skin was studied, when adrenaline,
noradrenaline and Octapressinm Sandoz were used as vasoconstrictors in local
anaesthetic agents injected subcutaneously on the forearms of healthy human
subjects.
During the first hour after injection changes in colour occurred in the adrenaline and noradrenaline zones : the initially pale zones became cyanotic.
The Octapressin zones remained pale throughout the entire experimental
period.
The tissue-oxygen tension, as measured with a Clark polyethylene membrane electrode attached to a Beckman Physiological Gas Analyzer, was clearly
lower after the administration of the catecholamines than after Octapressin.
The colour change and the decrease in tissue-oxygen tension are attributed
to the fact that the locally administered adrenaline or noradrenaline gives
rise to a local increase in oxygen consumption.
Since Octapressin, when used as a vasoconstrictor in local anaesthesia,
does not cause any local cyanosis, and the local tissue-oxygen tension is higher,
it may be more harmless to the tissue than the other two agents.

                              Z U SA M M E N FAS S U N G
    Die Gewebssauerstoffspannungder Haut am Unterarm gesunder Versuchs-
personen wurde nach subcutaner Injektion einer Lokalanaesthetikums unter
Zusatz von Adrenalin, Noradrenalin bzw. Octapressin (Sandoz) als Vasokon-
striktor gepriift.
    Innerhalb der ersten Stunde nach Injektion treten in den Adrenalin- und
Noradrenalinzonen Farbveranderungen auf: die anfanglich blasse Zone wird
zyanotisch. Nicht so bei Octapressin, wo die blasse Farbe bestehen bleibt.
    Die Gewebssauerstoffspannung (gemessen mit Clark-Membranelektrode
und Beckman Physiological Gas Analyzer) lag in den Catecholaminzonen
tiefer als im Octapressinbereich.
    Die Farbanderung und der Abfall der Sauerstoffspannung im Gewebe
wird auf eine lokale Erhohung des Sauerstoffverbrauchs unter der Einwirkung
von Adrenalin bzw . Noradrenalin zuriickgefuhrt. - Da Octapressin, als Vaso-
konstriktor dem Lokalanaesthetikum zugesetzt, keine ortliche Zyanose verur-
sacht und die Sauerstoffspannung im Gewebe nicht so stark absinken lasst,
durfte es fur das Gewebe harmloser sein als die Catecholamine.
