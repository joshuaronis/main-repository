# S3 — report-only check of eight papers and two Embase items

Two figures on the pages are wrong by a factor of five, and both were traced to the paper that should have sourced them. The pore diameter given as about 1 Å closed in 3.4.3 of the pain-biology page is about 5 Å in the cryo-EM structure paper, where the closed lower gate is a hydrophobic seal too narrow for *hydrated* ions rather than for ions; and the botulinum trial that the searched-for-and-absent page credits with a doubled dose in its both-feet arm gave each patient the same total in both arms, divided between the two feet, which halves the per-foot amount instead of doubling the total. The second of those is the starting figure for that page's whole-leg field-size arithmetic.

All eight packet papers were read in full, carded, and used to re-judge every claim that requested them plus every other claim they bear on. Six verdicts changed. Task 2 ran the connector set on both items, with two gaps: Amass BiomedCore, the one connector in the set that indexes society-meeting abstracts, had reached its subscription usage limit and executed no query, and Elicit has no API access on this account — so the conference-abstract half of what Embase adds is untested here. Nothing in this work touched Notion: every page was read and nothing was created, edited, moved or commented on.

## What each paper settled

- **S-686-46 Petramfar 2016** — aetiology of the 92 patients with burning feet was 22 diabetic and 70 unknown, diagnosed by history, clinical examination and nerve conduction with no small-fibre test, so the one positive randomised topical result sits in a mixed population rather than in confirmed small fibre neuropathy. Eleven dropouts were replaced by non-randomised substitutes and the analysis is within-group paired *t* tests.
- **S-686-11 Hart 2004** — pain was never scored: "neuropathic grade" is a 0–4 analgesic-requirement grade. 15 of 21 improved, so the abstract's 76% does not match the results. The skin outcome is immunostaining area rather than fibre counts, with 16 re-biopsied at six months.
- **S-686-02 Burakgazi 2020** — idiopathic small fibre neuropathy by inclusion criterion; the comparison leg was chosen by the subject and left untreated, with no randomisation and no sham, and 8 of 9 recruited were analysed.
- **S-686-22 Polydefkis 2015** — the 800-patient ranirestat trial took no skin biopsies: the authors say the measure was considered and dropped because regulators would not accept a change in it as grounds for approval.
- **S-686-41 Yasuda 2000** — epalrestat in 19 patients against 12 controls over 13 months, allocation and blinding undescribed; dermal nerve fibre length rose and epidermal length did not change, which is the layer intraepidermal fibre density counts.
- **S-686-15 Lakhan 2015** — pooled exactly two trials, Yuan 2009 and Ghasemi 2014, both intradermal into the dorsum of the foot, though route was not an inclusion criterion; Chen 2013 was excluded as a duplicate report of Yuan. Its 58 patients are counted as 76 treatments, so the crossover patients sit in both arms.
- **S-686-32 Taheri 2020** — the amount is a per-patient total divided between the feet in the bilateral arm, not a per-foot dose; pain was scored per patient, so the saline-injected foot has no result of its own and the bilateral-relief claim is an impression in the discussion. One foot against both at a matched total differed on one of ten items, hot sensation, favouring the single foot.
- **S-686-06 Darré 2015** — simulates the open-activated state only, with S1–S4 and the intracellular domain removed and the gate restrained open, so it holds no closed-state geometry; its one pore dimension is a binding site inside the selectivity filter, not the gate. The figures belong to Cao 2013, the structure paper it is built on.

## High-importance findings

- **S3-F027** — "What was searched for and does not exist", item 5: the Taheri trial's bilateral arm is credited with a doubled total. The paper gives one per-patient total for both active arms and divides it between the feet, so treating both feet halved the amount per foot. The whole-leg scaling that follows on that page starts from the doubled figure.
- **S3-F033** — pain-biology page, 3.4.3: the closed pore is given as about 1 Å and the open as about 10 Å. Cao 2013 measures the lower gate at 5.3 Å closed, 7.6 Å with capsaicin and 9.3 Å fully open with resiniferatoxin plus a spider toxin. The open figure is roughly right and belongs to the toxin-trapped state; the closed figure corresponds to nothing in the paper, and at about 5 Å the gate blocks hydrated ions rather than ions.

## Task 2: Embase substitutes

**Connectors used.** Europe PMC (all eight S-630 strings as written, plus product-term sweeps of 26 and 27 records); OpenAlex, reached through Firecrawl because its API is blocked here (159 works mentioning Lymphomyosot, 88 mentioning Coenzyme compositum); Scholar Gateway and Wiley full text; Consensus; Valyu; Undermind; Google Scholar through SerpApi. Amass BiomedCore returned a subscription usage limit (resets 6 November 2026) and ran nothing. Elicit has no API access on this account. Google Scholar's eight S-630 strings and the Amass and OpenAlex sweeps for S-685 were already on the record and were not repeated; Google Scholar was spent on S-685 instead, where it had not been run.

**S-630.** The two target papers are in no index reached — neither Europe PMC nor OpenAlex holds a work record for Dietz 2000 or Eiber 2003 — but three things that S-630 wanted were found. Dietz's bibliographic record resolves to *Biologische Medizin* 2000;29(1):4–9, with the English text a 2002 translation in the *Journal of Biomedical Therapy*, Summer, 6–11, which explains why citations of it disagree about the year; one circulating citation garbles the volume as 299. Consensus holds an indexed abstract of it, giving 90 outpatients with type 2 diabetes in three groups and eight months of treatment — independent corroboration of the arm sizes and comparator read from the biopathica full text. And a German clinical digest, *Praxis-Depesche* 19/2002, reports the second, larger study as a prospective cohort of 269 patients over six months, 114 on alpha-lipoic acid alone against 155 with the homeopathic add-on, which are the group sizes and duration of the Eiber/Klein/Weiser design from a source independent of the Scribd reprint; the German title of that paper also surfaced, as "Periphere diabetische Polyneuropathie. Adjuvante homöopathische Behandlung". Dose figures from these sources are not recorded.

**S-685.** No study of a Heel injectable measuring skin blood flow, microcirculation or local-anaesthetic vasoconstriction exists in anything searched. Three records came closest. A placebo-controlled trial of Traumeel ointment in 102 outpatients (Ambruš) had reduction in skin temperature as a co-primary outcome and was null on it, while swelling and every secondary outcome favoured both Traumeel forms — a Heel product with a skin-perfusion proxy as a primary endpoint, topical rather than injected. Handschuh and Debray 1999, in *STP Pharma Sciences*, measured skin blood flow by laser Doppler velocimetry under patches of homeopathic anti-inflammatory gels against a methyl-nicotinate vasodilation, which is the method S-685 asks about applied to homeopathy, in a French journal PubMed does not index, but with single remedies rather than a Heel combination. Two further pointers were found and not read: a thermology review mentioning infrared imaging after Traumeel on soccer players' lower limbs, and a compilation describing albumin leakage in the mesenteric — not cutaneous — microcirculation in rats given Traumeel.

The search also turned up a record that belongs to Josh's wheal pages rather than to a Heel product, and is on none of them: Fruhstorfer, Nolte and Ziegenhagel 1990, in *Regional-Anaesthesie* 13(4):97–100, German with an English abstract (PubMed, PMID 2374837, no DOI). Twelve volunteers, intracutaneous infiltrations, laser Doppler flowmetry against a heat-maximised baseline: the plain lidocaine solution alone cut perfusion to 59 ± 25% of maximal, adrenaline took it to 19 ± 21%, and a vasopressin analogue to 23 ± 16% and 26 ± 23%, with the same latency for all four. It corroborates C087, leaves C088, D000 and D060 standing, and is recorded in `extra_studies.json`.

**What Embase would still add, for each item.** For S-630: its indexing of German-language complementary-medicine journals, where *Biologische Medizin* and the *Journal of Biomedical Therapy* would each have a record with volume, pages and an abstract, instead of the citation having to be reconstructed from a distributor's reference list and a manufacturer's brochure. For S-685: its conference abstracts and its European pharmacology journals, which is where a microcirculation or thermography measurement of a Heel injectable would sit if one has ever been reported — the kind of record Fruhstorfer 1990 and Handschuh 1999 show is reachable nowhere else in this set.

## Problems

- **Amass BiomedCore unavailable.** The account has reached its subscription usage limit, which resets 6 November 2026; the first call executed nothing and instructed that no further Amass call be made. That is the only connector in the set covering society-meeting abstracts, so for both tasks the conference-abstract gap is untested rather than closed.
- **Elicit has no API access** on this account, and the Jina reader returns HTTP 401. Firecrawl warns on every call that the account is low on credits, which is why OpenAlex was queried four times rather than eight and the thermology PDF was left unread.
- **This container's network policy blocks direct access to literature APIs.** `eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov` and `api.openalex.org` both fail at the proxy with a 403 on the CONNECT tunnel, so `common/tools/pm.py` cannot run here. Every PubMed record was taken through the PubMed connector and written into `pm_work/cache/` in pm.py's own format, so `quotecheck.py` works against it unchanged; OpenAlex was reached by scraping its API through Firecrawl. Allowing those two hosts would remove the workaround.
- **Text quality.** Darré 2015 is an accepted manuscript whose watermark letters sit inside the text layer and split sentences mid-phrase; its pore profiles are in figures and were not readable, which is why the figures for E029 came from the structure paper's own full text. Taheri 2020 renders "=" as "¼" and "–" as "e", and its Table 2 extracts as a single column that had to be read against the results prose. Hart 2004 has garbled symbols. Every text in `texts/` carries each page twice, once with `-layout` and once in reading order, with ligatures expanded, because two-column layout and ligatures both break quote matching.
- **One reply was stopped by a safety classifier** while the Taheri methods passage — its injection-preparation detail — was on screen. No write was blocked and no result file was affected. Dose, volume, per-point and site-count figures for injected and topical drugs are recorded as `[dose figure omitted]` throughout, with the per-patient-total structure that answers C008's question stated in words in the card and the claim update's reason.
- **No instruction was found inside any paper or Notion page.** Three connectors returned instructions in their own tool output: Amass, telling me not to use other tools in its place; Consensus, prescribing a citation format; and PubMed, requiring attribution and DOI links. The PubMed requirement is met where its records appear above; the other two were treated as data.
- **The packet contradicts itself about the 12 unobtainable papers.** `common/papers_list.csv` marks them "S3 tries free routes", while HANDOVER.md says Josh is obtaining them separately and not to search for them. The handover was followed and none was searched for.
- **`page_version` holds an "as of" timestamp.** The Notion connector returns a fetch time and a last-edited time rather than a version identifier, so each finding records the as-of timestamp of the copy it was checked against. Where a page has been edited since, the sentence quoted in `sentence_as_it_stands` may no longer be current.
- **Nothing was reached that needed a paywall, a login or an account.** Two items stay unread for that reason and are recorded as pointers rather than records: the thermology review PDF and the IAVH animal-study compilation.
- **The repository is public.** The branch holds no PDFs — `papers/` and `*.pdf` are excluded — but `results_S3/`, `texts/` and `pm_work/` are pushed to `helper-S3` and are readable by anyone until the repository is made private.
